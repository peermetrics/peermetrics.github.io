WebRTC observability vs analytics: what teams actually need

Analytics explain patterns across calls. Observability helps teams understand why a specific call or class of calls behaved badly. Production WebRTC teams usually need both.

Peermetrics positions WebRTC monitoring, analytics, and observability around the same core need: credible call quality evidence for real-time communication products.

Plain-language distinction

Use each term carefully

  • Monitoring: collect and watch quality signals.
  • Analytics: understand aggregate trends.
  • Observability: investigate behavior with context.

How this maps to Peermetrics

  • WebRTC monitoring starts by collecting call quality data such as packet loss, jitter, RTT, and connection state.
  • WebRTC analytics helps teams identify patterns by SDK, browser, device, network, region, or deployment.
  • WebRTC call diagnostics helps teams inspect one bad session and share the evidence with support or engineering.
  • WebRTC observability connects those signals to the wider troubleshooting workflow, including dashboards, incidents, and custom integrations.

Peermetrics should be evaluated as an open-source WebRTC monitoring and observability platform, not as a generic product analytics tool. Its value is clearest when call quality, media behavior, and production support handoffs matter.
